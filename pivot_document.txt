PIVOT DOCUMENT — TURNING A SMALL SUM INTO A LARGE SUM
=======================================================
An honest assessment of what actually has to change, with the
arithmetic shown. Written 2026-08-24.

Premise being tested: "an agent that can convert a small sum of money
into a large sum based on reliable data and trading," starting from
about Rs 2,00,000, with the option to change asset class.

I am going to be blunt where the numbers are blunt. That is the most
useful thing I can do here.


==========================================================
PART 1 — THREE FINDINGS THAT CHANGE THE PLAN
==========================================================

FINDING 1: THE COST MODEL BREAKS AT SMALL POSITION SIZES
----------------------------------------------------------
factory.py charges COST_PER_SIDE = 0.0019 (0.38% round trip),
proportional to position size. Real Indian equity costs are NOT purely
proportional. They have a FIXED component.

Actual round-trip cost on an Indian equity delivery trade (discount
broker, delivery brokerage Rs 0):

  Variable:  STT 0.20% + exchange txn ~0.006% + stamp 0.015%
             + GST on charges ~0.001%   ==>  ~0.222% of position
  Fixed:     DP charge on sell          ==>  ~Rs 15.34 per scrip

  True round-trip cost % = 0.222% + (15.34 / position_size) x 100

Worked out:

  Position size      True round-trip cost      Model assumes
  --------------     ---------------------     -------------
  Rs   2,000              0.99%                    0.38%
  Rs  10,000              0.375%                   0.38%   <- only
  Rs  33,000              0.27%                    0.38%      here
  Rs  50,000              0.253%                   0.38%
  Rs 2,00,000             0.230%                   0.38%

The model is only accurate at roughly Rs 10,000 per position. Below
that it UNDERSTATES costs badly.

Why this matters enormously:

  * LADDER rung 1 = Rs 10,000. An event_drift cement strategy holds 6
    laggards. Rs 10,000 / 6 = Rs 1,667 per position. True round-trip
    cost ~1.14% — THREE TIMES what the model charges.
  * The first real-money rung is, as designed, economically
    non-viable for a basket strategy. A strategy promoted to rung 1
    would face triple the friction it was validated against.
  * If Rs 2 lakh were spread across 20 contestants x ~5 names = 100
    positions of Rs 2,000, the real cost is ~1% round trip against a
    modelled 0.38%. The project's own founding research showed that a
    correctly-specified strategy still LOST money to a 0.38% cost at
    30 round trips/year. At 1%, nothing survives.

Conclusion: the tournament architecture (many parallel strategies) is
excellent for GENERATING EVIDENCE and terrible for DEPLOYING CAPITAL at
Rs 2 lakh. These must be separated. Paper-trade many; deploy few, and
concentrated.


FINDING 2: THE COMPOUNDING MATH ON Rs 2 LAKH
----------------------------------------------
Rs 2,00,000 compounded, net of all costs and taxes:

  Net CAGR    After 5 yrs    After 10 yrs    Years to Rs 1 crore
  --------    -----------    ------------    -------------------
   12%         Rs  3.52 L     Rs  6.21 L          34.5 years
   18%         Rs  4.57 L     Rs 10.47 L          23.6 years
   25%         Rs  6.10 L     Rs 18.63 L          17.5 years
   35%         Rs  8.97 L     Rs 40.24 L          13.0 years
   50%         Rs 15.19 L     Rs 115 L             9.6 years

For calibration: Nifty long-run CAGR is ~12-13%. A sustained 25% net is
excellent-professional territory. A sustained 35%+ over a decade at
retail scale is essentially unheard of without leverage — and this
project's own Monte Carlo showed 10x leverage on a genuinely profitable
18%/yr strategy produces a MEDIAN outcome of losing 80%, with 54.5% of
accounts wiped out.

Now the contribution lever. Rs 2 lakh initial, at 18% net:

  Annual addition     Value at 10 yrs     Value at 20 yrs
  ---------------     ---------------     ---------------
  Rs 0                  Rs 10.5 L           Rs  54.8 L
  Rs 50,000             Rs 22.2 L           Rs 128 L
  Rs 1,00,000           Rs 34.0 L           Rs 201 L
  Rs 2,00,000           Rs 57.5 L           Rs 348 L

Read that table carefully. Adding Rs 50,000/year more than DOUBLES the
10-year outcome. Adding Rs 2 lakh/year produces a better result at 10
years than a zero-contribution account achieves in 20.

This is arithmetic, not opinion: AT A SMALL CAPITAL BASE, THE
CONTRIBUTION RATE DOMINATES THE RETURN RATE. Chasing 35% instead of
18% is far harder, far riskier, and worth less than simply feeding the
account from income.

The uncomfortable implication: if the goal is genuinely "small sum to
large sum," the highest-expectancy lever available is not a better
algorithm. It is (a) a validated modest edge, plus (b) aggressive
capital contribution from other income, plus (c) time.


FINDING 3: THE INFRASTRUCTURE PLAN IS INVERTED
------------------------------------------------
The stated plan is: profits fund cloud computing, then cloud compute
funds a local machine, and so on. At Rs 2 lakh this runs backwards.

Sensible rule: recurring infrastructure should stay under ~10% of
expected annual NET profit. At 18% net:

  Capital        Annual profit    Max sane infra spend
  --------       -------------    --------------------
  Rs   2 L        Rs  36,000       Rs   300 / month
  Rs   5 L        Rs  90,000       Rs   750 / month
  Rs  10 L        Rs 1.80 L        Rs 1,500 / month
  Rs  25 L        Rs 4.50 L        Rs 3,750 / month
  Rs  50 L        Rs 9.00 L        Rs 7,500 / month
  Rs   1 Cr       Rs 18.0 L        Rs 15,000 / month

Reference costs:
  GitHub Actions (private repo)  : FREE — 2,000 min/month.
                                   Current usage ~90 min/month.
                                   Headroom: ~22x.
  Hetzner CX22 VPS               : ~Rs 380/month
  DigitalOcean basic droplet     : ~Rs 500/month
  AWS t4g.small                  : ~Rs 1,000/month
  Zerodha Kite Connect API       : Rs 2,000/month  (= Rs 24,000/yr,
                                   which is 12% of the entire Rs 2 L
                                   capital — absurd at this size)
  Upstox / Dhan / Fyers APIs     : FREE
  yfinance / broker historical   : FREE

THE CURRENT STACK ALREADY COSTS Rs 0 AND HAS 22x HEADROOM.

There is no compute problem to solve. This workload is a few minutes of
pandas per day. You do not need cloud infrastructure until roughly
Rs 25 lakh of capital, and you do not need your own machine for this
workload essentially ever — a local machine helps only if you move to
latency-sensitive or genuinely heavy ML work, neither of which this
project's own research supports pursuing.

Every rupee spent on infrastructure before ~Rs 25 lakh is a rupee taken
directly out of compounding at the point where compounding is most
fragile.


==========================================================
PART 2 — ASSET CLASS: SHOULD WE CHANGE?
==========================================================
Evaluated specifically for a Rs 2 lakh, India-resident, systematic,
low-frequency operator.

A) INDIAN EQUITY DELIVERY  (current)
   +  Free APIs, free data, pipeline already built and running
   +  LTCG 12.5% if held >12 months, plus Rs 1.25 L/yr LTCG exemption
   +  No leverage, no margin calls, no liquidation risk
   +  Capacity is a non-issue at retail size
   -  20% STCG on holds under 12 months
   -  Rs ~15 DP charge per scrip per sell-day punishes small positions
   -  Market hours only; capital idle 5/7 days
   VERDICT: Correct choice for the evidence phase. Keep it. But
   CONCENTRATE — this only works if positions are >= ~Rs 25,000.

B) INDIAN F&O (futures / options)
   -  Post-Oct-2024 SEBI rules: lot sizes raised to ~Rs 15 lakh
      notional, weekly expiries cut to one per exchange, upfront
      premium collection, calendar-spread margin benefit removed on
      expiry day.
   -  ONE Nifty lot now requires roughly Rs 1.5-2 lakh margin. At
      Rs 2 lakh capital you can hold exactly one position, with zero
      diversification and zero margin buffer. A single gap against you
      is account-ending.
   -  Taxed as non-speculative business income at slab (up to ~31.2%).
   -  SEBI's own study found ~93% of individual F&O traders lost money
      over FY22-FY24, with average losses around Rs 2 lakh — i.e. the
      average loser lost this project's entire proposed capital.
   -  Directly contradicts this project's own leverage Monte Carlo.
   VERDICT: AVOID. Structurally unsuitable at this capital, and the
   evidence base is overwhelmingly negative.

C) CRYPTO (as an India resident)
   +  24/7 markets, fractional sizing completely solves the
      position-size problem, genuine inefficiencies at small size
   -  30% flat tax on gains, plus cess
   -  NO loss set-off — losing trades cannot offset winning trades
   -  1% TDS on EVERY transfer. This is the killer: a strategy doing
      30 round trips/year pays ~30% of turnover in TDS. It is
      creditable against final tax liability, but the cash-flow drag
      strangles a small account, and credit is capped by actual tax
      owed.
   -  No loss offset + 1% per-transaction TDS is mathematically hostile
      to any systematic strategy with meaningful turnover.
   -  Offshore exchanges sit in a FEMA grey zone for residents.
   VERDICT: The tax structure specifically penalises exactly what this
   system does. Viable only for very low-frequency positional holding.
   Not a fit for the tournament model.

D) US EQUITIES via LRS  <-- THE ONE GENUINELY WORTH EVALUATING
   +  FRACTIONAL SHARES ELIMINATE FINDING 1 ENTIRELY. You can hold a
      Rs 2,000 position with no fixed DP-style charge. The single
      biggest structural problem with Rs 2 lakh disappears.
   +  Near-zero commissions at several brokers; deeper liquidity;
      vastly better free data and research
   +  Rs 2 lakh is far below the $250,000/yr LRS limit and below the
      Rs 10 lakh/yr TCS threshold — neither binds at this size
   +  Historical INR depreciation has been a tailwind for Indian
      holders of USD assets
   -  Schedule FA disclosure in the ITR is MANDATORY. Non-disclosure
      carries Black Money Act penalties that dwarf any trading gain.
      This is a compliance requirement, not an optional step.
   -  Taxed as unlisted foreign shares: slab rate under 24 months,
      12.5% LTCG beyond 24 months — a longer LTCG runway than Indian
      equity
   -  Currency conversion spread on the way in and out
   -  Het's stated domain edge (Indian cement/infra/steel) does NOT
      transfer. You would be trading without your edge.
   VERDICT: Structurally superior for small capital. Strategically
   weaker, because it abandons the only genuine advantage claimed. Do
   NOT switch during the evidence phase. Reconsider it as the SCALING
   venue if and only if a real edge is proven.

E) INDIAN INDEX ETF (buy and hold)
   This is the benchmark, not a strategy. ~12-13% long-run, near-zero
   effort, near-zero cost, 12.5% LTCG.
   VERDICT: This is the number to beat. If the tournament cannot beat
   a Nifty ETF after all costs and taxes, the honest conclusion is to
   buy the ETF. Building this system is only justified if it clears
   that bar — and that comparison must be run explicitly.


==========================================================
PART 3 — WHAT THE PIVOT ACTUALLY IS
==========================================================

The binding constraints are NOT strategy sophistication. They are:
   1. Fixed transaction costs vs. a small capital base
   2. An evidence horizon of 35 days — statistically meaningless
   3. Infrastructure ambition inverted relative to capital

Therefore:

PIVOT 1 — STOP BUILDING MECHANISMS. START ACCUMULATING EVIDENCE.
  Three breeding mechanisms, an advisor ensemble, and a self-tuning
  trust weight now sit on top of 35 days of noise. Every additional
  evolutionary mechanism is another channel through which selection
  pressure can overfit a short sample — even when no single component
  "mines" data. The machinery is well ahead of the evidence.
  Freeze feature development. Let it run. The next genuinely valuable
  event is the calendar, not a commit.

PIVOT 2 — SEPARATE THE EVIDENCE ENGINE FROM THE CAPITAL ENGINE.
  Run 20-40 strategies on paper (free, parallel, high information).
  Deploy real capital to AT MOST 1-3, concentrated, with position
  sizes >= Rs 25,000. Raise LADDER rung 1 from Rs 10,000 to something
  economically coherent — OR require that rung-1 strategies hold at
  most 1-2 names. As designed, rung 1 loses to friction before it
  begins.
  (Note: LADDER is a financial risk parameter. Changing it requires
  Het's explicit, separate instruction. Flagging, not doing.)

PIVOT 3 — FIX THE COST MODEL BEFORE TRUSTING ANY VERDICT.
  Make COST_PER_SIDE size-aware: variable component plus a fixed
  per-scrip component. Until then, every promotion verdict is computed
  against costs that are too low at exactly the sizes being proposed.
  This is a correctness issue, not an optimisation.

PIVOT 4 — BENCHMARK AGAINST THE ETF, EXPLICITLY AND CONTINUOUSLY.
  Add Nifty buy-and-hold as a permanent, unkillable contestant in the
  tournament. If nothing beats it after 12 months of honest paper
  trading, that is a real and valuable answer — it means the correct
  action is to buy the index and put the energy elsewhere. A system
  that cannot produce that answer is not an evidence machine.

PIVOT 5 — TREAT CONTRIBUTION AS THE PRIMARY GROWTH LEVER.
  Per Finding 2, Rs 50,000/year of contribution beats improving the
  strategy from 18% to 25% net, and is enormously more achievable and
  more certain. The trading system's real job at this stage is not to
  generate wealth — it is to prove that an edge exists, so that larger
  capital can later be deployed with justified confidence.

PIVOT 6 — SPEND NOTHING ON INFRASTRUCTURE UNTIL ~Rs 25 LAKH.
  The stack costs Rs 0 today with 22x headroom. Revisit at the capital
  gates in the Finding 3 table, not before.


==========================================================
PART 4 — WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY FALSIFY THIS PROJECT
==========================================================
An evidence machine must be able to return "no." These are the
conditions under which the honest answer is to stop:

  * After 12 months of live paper trading, no strategy clears the
    existing RULES bar (126 days, 10 trades, 0.05%/day expectancy,
    Sharpe 0.4, drawdown better than -12%).
  * After 12 months, the best contestant does not beat a Nifty ETF
    net of the corrected (size-aware) cost model and applicable tax.
  * Promoted strategies systematically underperform their paper
    results once real costs apply — i.e. paper does not predict live.
  * The advisor trust weight decays to its floor (0.05), meaning
    advisor-evolved children consistently fail to beat the parents
    they replaced.

Write these down now, before the data arrives. That is the whole point
of Law 1, applied to the project itself rather than to a strategy.
